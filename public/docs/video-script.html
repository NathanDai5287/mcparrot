Demo Video Script

This page is the complete plan for the MCParrot demo video: what to say, what to show, and how to record and edit it.

Main cut 2:52 · two-minute cut 2:05 · YouTube link due with Devpost at 12:00pm PDT

The Browser Use judges may never stop at our table, so this video is often the only demo they see. It has to make one idea obvious in under three minutes: you teach MCParrot a website once, and then ChatGPT uses that website for you through Browser Use cloud browsers. The script opens on the strongest result, explains the problem, shows the setup quickly, and spends most of its time on two moments that only Browser Use makes possible. Every line below is written to be read aloud word for word, and every scene says exactly what to record. The Devpost submission page holds the written description that goes beside the video.

Video Rules From LA Hacks

The submission rules in the LA Hacks guide are the home for these facts, and this script is planned around them. The video must run two to three minutes and must be a YouTube link. The organizers ask for a short, clean video that is uploaded early, because a fresh upload plays in low quality until YouTube finishes processing it. Submissions close at 12:00pm PDT on October 18, 2026 with no late entries. They also suggest a Devpost cover picture with a 5:3 shape, which we can take from the opening frame of the video.

Sponsor judges may judge completely from Devpost, so this video aims at the Browser Use challenge first. The organizers recommend a "demo path" story that moves from a problem, to a moment of tension, to the resolution, and they advise showing features instead of describing them. The script follows that advice, so it never stops to explain architecture for long.

Story Built on Browser Use

The story has six beats, and Browser Use appears in three of them. It starts with the payoff, a ChatGPT answer that combines two websites, so the viewer knows within eight seconds why they should keep watching. It then shows the everyday problem: assistants cannot get into the websites students use most. The setup follows in fast steps, where Nathan records a website once with MCParrot Explorer, Claude finds the tools, and Nathan signs in once in a Browser Use cloud browser. The two big moments come next, both running on Browser Use: one question answered from Gradescope and Ed together, and an automatic sign-in where ChatGPT asks for a text-message code. The video ends with a short look under the hood and an invitation to try the app.

  1. Hook with the result

    Open on the midterm answer: 74 out of 101, a class mean of 52.14, and a z-score of 0.81.

  2. Show the problem

    Show the Canvas clicking routine and ChatGPT's lack of access to it.

  3. Teach the website once

    Record with MCParrot Explorer, then let Claude suggest tools.

  4. Sign in once with Browser Use

    Show the Browser Use live view inside the sign-in window.

  5. Let ChatGPT do the work

    Show the two-website answer, then the automatic sign-in with a phone code.

  6. Close with how and where

    Name the key technical choices, then show the address and the marketplace.

The Two-Minute Cut

The rules accept any length from two to three minutes, so a shorter version is still valid. Use it if the main cut runs over three minutes after editing, or if the morning runs short and some scenes have no good take. The two-minute cut keeps the hook and both Browser Use moments untouched, because they carry the Browser Use challenge. It removes or shortens only the setup and the closing explanation. Keep the same voiceover lines except where the table says otherwise.

Storyboard Frames

These two ChatGPT answers are real results from Nathan's own connectors, and the video builds toward them. Re-record them on recording day so the footage shows the whole run, but use these frames to plan zooms, callouts, and timing. The first frame is the hook and the first big moment. The second frame is the automatic sign-in moment; the code in it is blurred here, and it must be blurred in the video too. The first frame also makes a good Devpost thumbnail when cropped to a 5:3 shape around the score and the statistics table.

Keeping Every Claim Honest

Judges penalize polish that hides parts that do not work, so the video shows only real runs and says nothing the status guide does not support. Three scenes show capabilities that the status guide lists as a gap until a live run passes: recording a website with MCParrot Explorer, signing in through the Browser Use live view, and the text-message code step. Recording day is that live run, so film each of these for real and keep a take only if it works from start to finish. In the two-website scene, open ChatGPT's "Worked for" details after the run and check that it called the image reading tool; if it did not, replace "including the class chart, which Claude reads straight from the image" with "including the class statistics". The video does not mention ordering, signing back in with Google, or management from ChatGPT, because none of them has passed a live run.

Every speed ramp carries a small "sped up" label, and the two long waits show their real time, so no viewer thinks the answers are instant. Blur every private value before export: passwords, the connection link, verification codes, email addresses, and other students' names. Close ChatGPT's chat history sidebar so old conversation titles never appear. Never show the Browser Use, Clerk, or Sentry dashboards with keys visible.

Recording the Screen

Record at 1920 × 1080 at 60 frames a second, or at 4K if the editor can handle it, because a 4K recording lets the edit zoom in without blur. Set the browser zoom to 125 percent so ChatGPT and the dashboard text stay readable on a phone screen. Use a separate, clean Chrome profile that has only Canvas, Gradescope, Ed, ChatGPT, and MCParrot signed in. Hide the bookmarks bar, unpin every extension except MCParrot Explorer, hide desktop icons, and turn on macOS Do Not Disturb so no notification appears. A recorder with cursor highlighting and automatic zoom, such as Screen Studio, saves editing time; OBS or QuickTime also work, with zooms added in the editor.

Waits are the enemy of a short video, so reduce them before recording and cut them afterwards. MCParrot reuses an app's Browser Use browser for up to 7 minutes after a tool call, so ask ChatGPT one throwaway question in a different chat a minute or two before each take to warm the browser. Record the Browser Use live view inside the sign-in window at full size, because it is the clearest picture of Browser Use in the whole video. During long waits, keep the cursor still and say nothing, which makes clean speed ramps easy. Record each scene as its own clip, starting two seconds before the action and stopping two seconds after it.

Recording the Voiceover

Record the voiceover separately, after the screen footage is cut together, so the words can match the picture exactly. Use a USB microphone or a headset microphone about a hand's width from the mouth, not the laptop's built-in microphone. Choose the quietest room available, and soft surroundings such as a closet full of clothes or a blanket over the desk remove most echo. Read each scene's line three times in a row, then keep the best take, and stand up while reading to sound more awake. Speak a little slower and with more energy than in conversation, since the pitch guide warns that a monotone or tired voice loses judges.

Add a quiet, upbeat instrumental music bed from the YouTube Audio Library, because copyrighted music can get the video blocked or muted. Keep the music about 25 decibels below the voice, and lower it further while the voice speaks. Let the music rise briefly only at the title card and the end card. Use a single sound effect at most, the whoosh into the fresh ChatGPT chat. Export audio at a loudness of about −14 LUFS, which is the level YouTube expects.

Editing the Final Cut

Burn captions into the video instead of relying on YouTube's subtitles, because many judges watch with the sound off. Use large white text with a dark shadow, at most two short lines, near the bottom of the frame and clear of the lower thirds. Zoom in to between 1.5 and 2 times on every key part of the interface: the Explorer badge, the suggested tools, the live view, the score, the statistics table, and the code request. Use the same style for every lower third, so the viewer learns where to look for feature names. The lower thirds name these features: MCParrot Explorer, Claude Opus 5.5 through the Cursor SDK, Browser Use cloud browser and live view, persistent profile, private ChatGPT connection link, and Browser Use agent.

Keep the pacing steady by changing the picture every three to six seconds, either with a cut or a zoom. Use hard cuts between scenes, one fade into the title card, and one fade into the closing diagram, and avoid fancy transitions. Match the end of each voiceover line to the moment its result appears on screen. Export as an MP4 with H.264 video at 1080p and 60 frames a second, or at 4K, and watch the whole export once on a phone before uploading.

Pre-Recording Checklist

Finish every item in this list before the first take, because fixing a setup problem in the middle of recording costs much more time. The checklist is complete when a test question works in each ChatGPT connector.

  • The clean Chrome profile is signed in to Canvas, Gradescope, Ed, ChatGPT, and the MCParrot dashboard.
  • MCParrot Explorer version 0.8.0 is installed in that profile, and its version shows 0.8.0 on the Chrome extensions page.
  • The Canvas, Gradescope, and Ed apps each have a working Browser Use sign-in, and a test tool call from ChatGPT returns a result.
  • ChatGPT has the Canvas, Gradescope, and Ed connectors added, and each take starts in a fresh chat with only the needed connectors on.
  • A spare Canvas app exists for filming the teaching scenes, so the real Canvas app stays untouched.
  • The phone that receives Canvas codes is charged, unlocked, and on the desk, with its notification previews visible for the B-roll shot.
  • The bookmarks bar is hidden, extra extensions are unpinned, desktop icons are hidden, and Do Not Disturb is on.
  • The browser zoom is 125 percent, and the screen recorder is set to 1920 × 1080 or 4K at 60 frames a second.
  • The microphone is plugged in, a 10-second test recording sounds clean, and this script is open on a second screen or a printout.

Recording Day Runbook

Nathan records and edits the video, and the work is complete when the YouTube link plays in high quality from the Devpost page. Submissions close at 12:00pm PDT, and YouTube needs time to process, so the upload must start by 11:00am. Keep at least two good takes of each Browser Use scene, because a website can sign out or slow down at the worst moment. If a take fails, warm the browser again and retry once, then move on and come back later. Follow these steps in order.

  1. By 8:00am, finish the pre-recording checklist.
  2. Record the problem montage and the Canvas, Gradescope, and Ed B-roll.
  3. Record the teaching scenes on the spare Canvas app: create the app, explore with MCParrot Explorer, and find tools.
  4. Record the sign-in scene with the Browser Use live view, then record creating and adding the connection link.
  5. Warm the Gradescope and Ed browsers with a throwaway question, then record the two-website question in a fresh chat at least twice.
  6. Sign the Canvas app out: select Sign in to website, use Canvas's own sign-out link in the live view, and select Done signing in.
  7. Record the ENGIN 125 question in a fresh chat, and type the phone code within two minutes, because a free Browser Use project stops the waiting browser after two minutes. If no take works, screen-record a slow scroll through the real conversation from October 17 instead.
  8. Record the phone close-up of the incoming code as separate B-roll.
  9. By 9:30am, cut the footage to the shot list, with speed ramps and "sped up" labels.
  10. Record the voiceover against the cut, then add music, captions, zooms, lower thirds, and blurs.
  11. Export, watch the whole video once on a phone, and confirm that it runs between 2:00 and 3:00.
  12. By 11:00am, upload to YouTube as Public or Unlisted, never Private, with the title "MCParrot: Your Personal Web Agent, Built on Browser Use".
  13. Wait until YouTube offers 1080p, then paste the link into Devpost and check that it plays in a private browser window.
  14. Crop the opening frame to 5:3 and upload it as the Devpost thumbnail.
